Tarotio

A daily reading for your sign

One reading a day, dealt for your sign.

Three hands of ten cards, face down in two rows. The reader gives you omens. Work out where the Full Moon hides in each hand, turn it over, and keep the three cards that become your fortune for today. The puzzle is the same for everyone; the cards you draw are your own.

  1. Read the omens. Each hand comes with a few true clues. Together they point to exactly one card: the Full Moon.
  2. Turn a card. Tap a card to pick it up, then turn it over.
  3. Win chips. Turn the Full Moon first and the reader gives you a chip. Spend it on a later hand to pass a card and try again.

Find all three Full Moons and your fortune is a Full Moon, every day. Luck only decides the hands you leave to chance.

Choose your sign

Your sign sets your deck, your own Major Arcana card, and the suit that shines brighter for you.

The whole hand

    Tarotio is a game for reflection and fun. The cards follow traditional Rider-Waite-Smith meanings. They are a mirror for your day, and your choices stay your own.

    How to play

    Each day one deck is shuffled for every star sign. It deals you three hands of ten cards, face down in two rows of five. You keep one card from each hand: your Past, Present and Future.

    1. Read the omens. Each hand comes with two to five omens. Every omen is true, and together they leave exactly one place the Full Moon can be. The Past is plain. The Future always needs omens chained together.
    2. Turn a card. Tap a card to pick it up, then tap it again or press Turn it over.
    3. Found it, or not. Turn the Full Moon and it is yours at once. Turn any other card and, if you hold a chip, you can keep it or spend the chip to pass and turn again. A passed card is burned: you cannot go back to it. With no chips, the card you turn is the card you keep.
    4. Win chips. Turn the Full Moon on your first try and the reader gives you a chip for the hands ahead. Chips carry over, so reading the Past and Present true gives you room on the Future.

    What you can see

    Above each hand is a row of ten moons: exactly what the hand holds, but never which card is which. The mark on the back of each card is its suit, or a star for the Major Arcana. The omens tie the two together.

    The omens use a few words for the grid. A card touches the cards beside it in its row and the card directly above or below it. The corners are the cards at both ends of both rows. The outer columns are the first and fifth. “The Half Moon” means the only card with that moon; the board shows when there is just one.

    Tap an omen to light up the cards it speaks of. Tap the label under a card to rule that card out; it is your pencil mark and changes nothing else.

    The moons

    Every card has a brightness for the day, from New Moon to Full Moon. It comes from the card’s traditional meaning and whether it lands upright or reversed. A reversed card is often dimmer, but the hardest cards turn kinder when reversed.

    No card is bad. New-moon cards are the ones that ask something of you.

    Your fortune

    Your fortune is the moons of the three cards you keep. Three Full Moons make 15 moons, a Full Moon reading. 13 or 14 is a Gibbous Moon, 11 or 12 a Half Moon, 9 or 10 a Crescent Moon.

    Everyone’s puzzle, your own cards

    Everyone gets the same puzzle each day, whatever their sign: the same moons, the same suit marks, the same omens, the Full Moons in the same places. Which tarot card lies in each place is drawn for you alone, always matching its suit mark and moon. Two players who find the same Full Moon may turn over The Sun and the Ace of Cups.

    Your sign shapes how your cards are read. Cards of your element and your sign’s own Major Arcana card speak to you most directly, and patterns across your three cards (three of a suit, a repeated number, a run) colour the reading. They never change the moons, so the same play always earns the same fortune.

    Skill and luck

    Every hand holds exactly one Full Moon, and the omens always lead to it. Find all three and your fortune is a Full Moon, every day. That part is skill.

    Luck comes in only where the omens get the better of you. A card turned on a guess can still be bright, and sometimes a guess lands on the Full Moon itself. Turning all three blind finds them about once in a thousand games.

    Your result shows both. Insight is how well you read the omens: two for each hand where your first turn was the Full Moon, one where you found it after passing. Fortune is the moons you kept, beside how often turning blind would have done as well as you did.

    Keyboard

    1 to 5 pick up a card in the top row, 6 to 9 and 0 in the bottom row; the same key or Enter turns it. Shift with the key rules a card out. K keeps, P passes.
