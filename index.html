Tarotio

A daily reading for your sign

One reading a day, dealt for your sign.

Three hands of five cards, face down. Turn a card, then hold your nerve: keep it, or pay a chip to pass and turn another. The three you keep become your fortune for today.

  1. Win chips. Every hand comes with one chip. Answer the reader’s question to win up to two more.
  2. Turn a card. Keep it, or pay a chip to pass and turn another. A passed card is gone for good.
  3. Read the table. The moons above each hand show what it holds, though never where. The mark on each back shows the card’s suit.

Choose your sign

Your sign sets your deck, your own Major Arcana card, and the suit that shines brighter for you.

Turned so far

    Tarotio is a game for reflection and fun. The cards follow traditional Rider-Waite-Smith meanings. They are a mirror for your day, and your choices stay your own.

    How to play

    Each day one deck is shuffled for every star sign. It deals you three hands of five cards, face down. You keep one card from each hand: your Past, Present and Future.

    1. Win chips. Every hand comes with one chip from the reader. Answer the question to win more: two for right first time, one for right on the second try. Chips you do not spend carry to the next hand.
    2. Turn a card. Tap any face-down card to turn it over.
    3. Keep or pass. Keep the card, or pay one chip to pass. A passed card is burned: you cannot go back to it. With no chips left, the card you turn is the card you keep.

    The moons

    Every card has a brightness for the day, from New Moon to Full Moon. It comes from the card’s traditional meaning and whether it lands upright or reversed. A reversed card is often dimmer, but the hardest cards turn kinder when reversed.

    No card is bad. New-moon cards are the ones that ask something of you.

    Reading the table

    Above each hand is a row of five moons: exactly what the hand holds, but never which card is which. As you turn cards, their moons are crossed off, so you can always see what is still face down before you risk a pass.

    The mark on the back of each card is its suit. Cards in your sign’s suit are worth more to you, and suits are how you build a hand.

    Building a hand

    Your three cards are read together, the way a reader would, and the right three are worth more than their moons:

    • Your element: each card in your sign’s suit, +1. Swords is the hardest suit, so Air signs get +2.
    • Your card: your sign’s own Major Arcana card, +2.
    • Trine (a flush): three of one suit, +2.
    • Fate’s Hand: three Major Arcana, +2.
    • Echo (a pair): two cards with the same number, +1. Three, +3.
    • The Stair (a straight): three numbers in a row, +2.

    A reading needs 11 moons for a Half Moon, 14 for a Gibbous Moon and 16 for a Full Moon.

    Skill and luck

    Your answers win chips, and chips let you dig deeper into a hand. But the deck is shuffled before you sit down. A perfect player can still be dealt a quiet day, and a first turn can still be The Sun.

    Your reading shows both parts. Insight is the chips you won. Fortune is what the cards gave. It also shows what your first turns alone would have made, so you can see exactly what your passes changed.

    Keyboard

    1 to 4 answer a question. 1 to 5 turn a card. K keeps, P passes.
